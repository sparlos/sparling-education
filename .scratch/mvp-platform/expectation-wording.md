# Expectation wording: drafts for review

Kindling's descriptions in `packages/curriculum/src/math-2020.ts` are shown to teachers beside each code: on the dashboard, in a cell's evidence and in quick assess. Each draft below describes the **whole** expectation in plain words, not only the part a game evidences. Each is checked against the copied-words rule (no run of 6 words shared with the official text), so it stays Kindling's own wording (ADR 0001).

For each: the official text (from `reference/`, for review only, never shipped), today's wording, and the draft. Approve, or edit the draft line directly.

**Status:** approved by the founder, 2026-10-29, and applied.

## Grade 3

### B1.6 (Fractions)

- **Official:** use drawings to represent, solve, and compare the results of fair-share problems that involve sharing up to 20 items among 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, and 10 sharers, including problems that result in whole numbers, mixed numbers, and fractional amounts
- **Today:** Shares a number of wholes equally among up to 10 people and describes each share, whether it comes out whole, fractional or as a mixed number.
- **Draft:** Solves fair-share problems with drawings: up to 20 items shared among 2 to 6, 8 or 10 people, with shares that come out as whole numbers, mixed numbers or fractions, and compares the results.

### B1.7 (Fractions)

- **Official:** represent and solve fair-share problems that focus on determining and using equivalent fractions, including problems that involve halves, fourths, and eighths; thirds and sixths; and fifths and tenths
- **Today:** Finds that two different fractions can name the same share, such as one half and four eighths, or one fifth and two tenths.
- **Draft:** Solves fair-share problems that show two fractions can be the same amount, using eighths, fourths and halves; sixths and thirds; or tenths and fifths.

### B2.8 (Multiplication and Division)

- **Official:** represent the connection between the numerator of a fraction and the repeated addition of the unit fraction with the same denominator using various tools and drawings, and standard fractional notation
- **Today:** Understands the top number of a fraction as a count of equal unit pieces, so three fifths is one fifth taken three times.
- **Draft:** Shows with tools, drawings and fraction notation that the numerator counts unit fractions: three fifths is one fifth added three times.

## Grade 4

### B1.4 (Fractions and Decimals)

- **Official:** represent fractions from halves to tenths using drawings, tools, and standard fractional notation, and explain the meanings of the denominator and the numerator
- **Today:** Shows fractions from halves to tenths with models and symbols, and explains that the bottom number sets the size of each part and the top number counts the parts.
- **Draft:** Shows fractions from halves to tenths with drawings, tools and fraction notation, and explains what the numerator and denominator each mean.

### B1.5 (Fractions and Decimals)

- **Official:** use drawings and models to represent, compare, and order fractions representing the individual portions that result from two different fair-share scenarios involving any combination of 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, and 10 sharers
- **Today:** Compares and orders the shares that come from two different equal-sharing situations, reasoning about how many wholes and how many people.
- **Draft:** Uses drawings and models to compare and order the share each person gets in two different fair-share problems, with 2 to 6, 8 or 10 sharers in each.

### B1.6 (Fractions and Decimals)

- **Official:** count to 10 by halves, thirds, fourths, fifths, sixths, eighths, and tenths, with and without the use of tools
- **Today:** Counts on by a unit fraction past one whole, for example one fourth, two fourths and on to eleven fourths, with or without a model.
- **Draft:** Counts up to 10 in unit fractions, halves to tenths (not sevenths or ninths), with or without tools: one fourth, two fourths, and on to forty fourths.

## Grade 5

### B1.3 (Fractions, Decimals, and Percents)

- **Official:** represent equivalent fractions from halves to twelfths, including improper fractions and mixed numbers, using appropriate tools, in various contexts
- **Today:** Makes and recognises equal-value fractions up to twelfths by splitting or merging parts, including fractions greater than one written as improper fractions or mixed numbers.
- **Draft:** Finds and shows fractions that are equal in value, from halves to twelfths, including ones greater than one (improper fractions and mixed numbers), with tools and in different situations.

### B1.4 (Fractions, Decimals, and Percents)

- **Official:** compare and order fractions from halves to twelfths, including improper fractions and mixed numbers, in various contexts
- **Today:** Puts fractions up to twelfths in order of size, those greater than one too (improper or mixed), using strategies like common parts, common counts and the one-half benchmark.
- **Draft:** Compares fractions from halves to twelfths and puts them in order of size, including ones greater than one, as improper fractions or mixed numbers, in different situations.

### B2.5 (Addition and Subtraction)

- **Official:** add and subtract fractions with like denominators, in various contexts
- **Today:** Adds and subtracts fractions that share a denominator by combining or comparing counts of the same unit piece.
- **Draft:** Adds and subtracts fractions that have the same denominator, in different situations.
